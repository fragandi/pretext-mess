 \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black]
    \begin{center}
    \title{{\large \bolder{MSCS Colloquium 12/1!
}}}
    \end{center}
\end{tcolorbox}

Join Alexander Wiedemann, a Computer Science faculty candidate, who will be giving an MSCS Department Colloquium talk titled “Algorithmics and Complexity of Genome Rearrangement” on Monday, December 1, 2025 at 3:30 PM in RNS 210! Wiedemann’s research sits at the intersection of theory and computation in network and data science, with current work spanning randomized and parameterized algorithms for genome rearrangement, probabilistic inference for opinion dynamics, and public interest data science with community partners. 

In his talk, he will explore the challenges of modeling genome rearrangements, which are large scale mutations that reorder genes within a genome, and discuss how balancing biological realism with computational feasibility leads to deep questions about counting, inference, and complexity. He will also highlight state of the art approaches for several genome rearrangement models, including recent results for the Single Cut and Join model. Everyone is welcome to attend!

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=white, colframe=black]
    \begin{center}
    \title{{\large \bolder{CSCI 121 Principles of Computer Science (J-term)
}}}
    \end{center}
\end{tcolorbox}

Dive into computer science with Professor Houssou! This introductory, hands-on course will develop your analytical thinking and problem-solving skills through daily programming exercises. You will apply programming concepts to real-world projects across various fields like science and engineering, as well as music, sociology, finance, art, and more. 

The smaller class size during J-term allows you a more personalized experience into the realm of computer science with one-on-one support from Professor Houssou and close collaboration with your classmates. This is also a great opportunity to build up your technology skills in an ever-increasing technical world.
